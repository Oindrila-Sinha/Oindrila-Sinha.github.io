Research

Why do people respond differently to the same practice? My work connects perception, prediction and action.

Anticipatory gaze and implicit sequence learning

Where we look reveals what we have learned — sometimes before our movements do. Combining eye tracking with a serial reaction time task, I showed that anticipatory gaze exposes individual differences in implicit sequence learning before motor performance changes.

Eye trackingMotor learningIndividual differences

Vision, prediction and posture

When we stop, catch or brace against a moving object, the brain prepares in advance. My doctoral work at Penn State showed that smooth pursuit eye movements and peripheral vision support anticipatory hand forces, predictive posture stabilization and long-latency reflexes.

Smooth pursuitPostureReflexes

Sensing real-world prosthesis use

Clinic tests don't show how people use an upper-limb prosthesis in daily life. With engineering collaborators, I use embedded soft sensors and machine learning to characterize functional prosthesis engagement in real-world contexts.

WearablesMachine learningProsthetics

Aging and predictive cognition

Aging selectively impairs how peripheral vision calibrates anticipatory postural responses. I'm now asking how aging changes predictive cognition during sequence learning — and how cognitive abilities explain who benefits from practice.
