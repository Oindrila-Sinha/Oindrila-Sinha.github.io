Research

Why do people respond differently to the same practice?

Anticipatory gaze and implicit sequence learning

Where we look reveals what we have learned — sometimes before our movements do. Combining eye tracking with a serial reaction time task, my work shows that anticipatory gaze reveals individual differences in implicit sequence learning before changes in motor performance appear.

Eye trackingMotor learningIndividual differences

Vision, prediction, and aging

When we catch, stop, or brace against a moving object, the brain prepares in advance. My doctoral work at Penn State showed that smooth pursuit eye movements and peripheral vision support anticipatory hand forces, predictive posture stabilization, and rapid reflex responses — and that aging selectively impairs how peripheral vision calibrates these responses.

Smooth pursuitPostureLong-latency reflexesAging

Sensing real-world prosthesis use

Clinic tests don't show how people actually use an upper-limb prosthesis at home. With engineering collaborators, I apply embedded soft sensors and machine learning to characterize functional prosthesis engagement in real-world contexts.

Biomedical sensing (earlier work)

Before my PhD, I developed sensing systems at the S. N.
