Research

Why do people respond differently to the same practice? My work connects perception, prediction and action.

01Perception → action

Gaze reveals learning before movement

Where we look reveals what we have learned — sometimes before our movements do. Combining eye tracking with a serial reaction time task, I showed that anticipatory gaze diverges well before reaction times do, and that learners cluster into perception-reliant and action-biased profiles.

02Vision & posture

Seeing ahead to stay stable

When we stop, catch or brace against a moving object, the brain prepares in advance. My doctoral work at Penn State showed that smooth pursuit and peripheral vision support anticipatory hand forces, predictive posture stabilization and long-latency reflexes.

03Wearables

Measuring prosthesis use in daily life

Clinic tests don't show how people use an upper-limb prosthesis in daily life. With engineering collaborators, I use embedded soft sensors and machine learning to characterize functional prosthesis engagement in real-world contexts (NSF DARE and NIH R01 proposals, Co-PI).

04Aging

Why older adults learn differently

Aging selectively impairs how peripheral vision calibrates anticipatory postural responses. I'm now asking how aging changes predictive cognition during sequence learning — and how cognitive abilities explain who benefits from practice (American Academy of Neurology proposal, PI).
